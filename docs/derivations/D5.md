## Assigned statement

> split(T, k), implemented as a sequence of joins, costs O(log n) in total because the heights telescope. Prove it, and construct an insertion order that forces rotations on a constant fraction of insertions.

## Why split takes logarithmic time

Let $H$ be the input height, with empty trees at height zero. In leaderboard order (decreasing score, then increasing ID), split returns $L$ before $k$ and $R$ at or after $k$. For $x<k$, split the right child and join the untouched left child, pivot $x$, and the recursive $L$. Carry $R$ back unchanged. For $x\ge k$, split the left child and rebuild $R$ symmetrically.

**Join bound.** By D1, joining heights $a$ and $b$ costs $O(1+|a-b|)$. The result has height between $\max(a,b)$ and $1+\max(a,b)$. Join descends the taller spine until the heights differ by at most one. Each step drops at most two levels, so the subtree it reaches is at least as tall as the shorter input. Adding the pivot grows that subtree by one. On the way back, AVL insertion rebalancing leaves each ancestor at its old height or one level taller.

**Charge each join to one output.** First, by induction, neither split output exceeds the height of its input. The empty case is immediate. In the recursive case, the carried output is at most the searched child's height. The rebuilt output is at most one plus the maximum of the two original child heights by the join bound. Both heights are therefore at most the original node's height.

At one return step, let $z$ and $z'$ be the rebuilt output's heights before and after the join, and $s$ the untouched sibling's height. AVL balance and the induction bound give $z\le s+1$. If $z>s$, the height gap is at most one. If $s\ge z$, then $z'\ge s$, so the gap $s-z$ is at most $z'-z$. Also $z'\ge z$ by the join bound. Thus the cost of this step is $O(1+z'-z)$.

**Sum the charges.** Both output heights start at zero. Each step grows at most one of them, so the sum of the charged increases telescopes separately on the two outputs:

$$
\sum_{\text{joins}}(z'-z) = h(L)+h(R) \le 2H.
$$

There are at most $H$ steps. Including the search, the total cost is $O(H)=O(\log n)$ for $n\ge2$.

In `avl_split_by_key`, cutting at ID 8 with score 90 puts ID 1 at score 90 in $L$ and ID 8 in $R$. A score-only cut at 90 puts both in the high board. The merged implementation uses `splitKey` for key cuts and `splitScore` for score cuts. Both rebuild the outputs with `joinNodes` in `src/structA/avl.cpp`.

## An insertion order with frequent rotations

Insert $n$ keys in increasing tree order, using decreasing distinct scores as in the `descending` case of `generate` in `adversary/generator.cpp`. Every insertion appends a node to the right spine. If an ancestor first becomes unbalanced, the growth was in its right child's right subtree. For left subtree height $a$, the ancestor's right subtree grew from $a+1$ to $a+2$. A single left rotation makes the two children of the new root both height $a+1$, restoring the old subtree height $a+2$. This prevents further rotations above it.

Every left rotation on the right spine removes one node from that spine. With $r$ rotations after $n$ insertions, the spine length is exactly $n-r$. The spine fits inside the final tree height $H$, so $r\ge n-H$. Each rotating insertion accounts for exactly one rotation. Since an AVL has $H=O(\log n)$, at least half the insertions rotate for all sufficiently large $n$.
