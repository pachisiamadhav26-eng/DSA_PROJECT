# D1 Ordered AVL join

Module owner: Prince Chavda

Let L and R be AVL trees, and let every key in L precede pivot p and every
key in R follow p. The tree order is decreasing score, then increasing ID.
Write h(T) for stored height, with h(empty) = 0.

## Algorithm and order

If the input heights differ by at most one, attach L and R as the pivot's two
children. Otherwise descend the right spine of the taller L, or the left
spine of the taller R. When returning, reconnect the joined subtree and
rebalance each visited ancestor. On the taller L side, every key on the
untouched left side already precedes that ancestor, and all newly attached
keys follow it. On the taller R side the reasoning is symmetric. A rotation
preserves the in-order sequence, so the entire result is L, p, R.

## Height and metadata

Inductively, the join result has height between max(h(L),h(R)) and that
maximum plus one. The base case has height exactly maximum plus one and
balance at most one. Suppose L is taller by at least two. Its right child is
at least h(L)-2, hence at least h(R). The recursive result can only increase
the right child's height by at most one. Its sibling is unchanged. The
ancestor is still balanced or is now right-heavy by exactly two.

In that second case the old right child was strictly taller than R. If its
join is a base case, the pivot receives two child heights differing by one.
Otherwise, a height increase requires an unrotated root whose once-equal
children now differ by one. Inductively its two child heights therefore
differ by one. An outer-heavy child calls for
one left rotation. An inner-heavy child calls for a right rotation on that
child followed by a left rotation on the ancestor. Put a = height of the
ancestor's unchanged left subtree. The right child then has height a+2.
In the outer-heavy case its inner and outer subtree heights are a and a+1.
After rotation the old root's height is a+1 and the new root's height is a+2.
In the inner-heavy case the middle subtree has height a+1, with children's
heights in {a-1,a} (using zero for empty trees). The two demoted roots both
end at height a+1 and the promoted root ends at a+2. These are balanced and
restore the ancestor's previous height. The taller R case is the mirror
image. Refresh each changed node after its children, using
size(v) = 1 + size(left) + size(right). This maintains all subtree sizes.

## Cost

Each descent reduces the taller height by at least one, and it stops when
heights are within one. Returning takes constant time per visited ancestor
for metadata and at most two local rotations. Time and call-stack space are
O(1 + |h(L)-h(R)|). Pivot allocation is constant work. A league merge has
extra work to find an ordered pivot or combine overlapping ranges, so its
cost belongs in a separate measurement.
